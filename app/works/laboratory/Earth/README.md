# Earth

Real time mode defaults to Suwon (37.2636° N, 127.0286° E), with an assumed observation elevation of 40 m. Astronomy Engine 2.1.19 (MIT) computes topocentric Sun/Moon vectors and rotates the J2000 star catalogue into the observer's east/north/up frame. The clock follows the device time, refreshed every second even when cloud animation is paused. The menu uses Asia/Seoul (UTC+9) explicitly; date previews accept 1900–2100 and Now restores live time. Latitude/longitude are editable. Solar irradiance and apparent solar radius follow Earth–Sun distance; heliocentric ecliptic coordinates, distance, and longitude are shown in the menu.

Stars down to magnitude 6 and optional constellation lines come from d3-celestial (Olaf Frohn, BSD-3-Clause; underlying star data: XHIP, Anderson & Francis 2012, VizieR V/137D). The atlas uses J2000 catalogue coordinates, magnitude-based display brightness, and approximate B−V colors. Daily rotation and precession/nutation are included by the coordinate transform; individual stellar proper motion is omitted. A single RGBA atlas packs stars and lines. Daylight/twilight suppresses them; atmospheric transmission, ground, and volumetric clouds occlude them.

The Moon uses topocentric distance for angular radius and the Moon-to-Sun vector for Lambertian reflected light, so position, phase, and bright-limb orientation vary with time/location. Surface markings are procedural, not a lunar map. Refraction, detailed lunar libration/topography, lunar eclipses, lunar illumination of clouds/ground, local terrain, city light pollution, and real weather are not simulated. This is a sky visualization, not an observing ephemeris or weather service. The original manual presets remain available.

Rayleigh and Mie scattering sliders scale the atmosphere's actual RGB scattering coefficients. Mie extinction scales with Mie scattering, preserving its albedo. Editing either coefficient rebuilds transmittance, single Rayleigh/Mie scattering, and first-order sky irradiance lookup textures on the GPU. The unchanged 1.00 settings use Bruneton's shipped higher-order lookup textures and preserve Air parity. Edited settings use the rebuilt single-scattering solution; higher-order atmospheric scattering is not recomputed. The cloud model continues to use the rebuilt transmittance and irradiance textures.

Cloud patterns combine domain-warped weather at three scales, independently rotated detail volumes with different periods, and larger cloud clusters. These vary cloud boundaries, density, and height without changing the field between Orbit and local views. Distance-based mip filtering remains active; empty weather regions skip detail lookups.

Combines Air's Eric Bruneton atmospheric scattering LUTs with Cloud's periodic volume-noise approach in one WebGL2 renderer. Existing Air and Cloud studies remain accessible.

Lighting uses the same world-space sun for the atmosphere, surface, and clouds. Cloud density integrates Beer–Lambert extinction along the camera ray; a six-sample light ray estimates self-shadowing. A dual Henyey–Greenstein phase function provides forward scattering and a weaker backscattering lobe. Direct sunlight is attenuated using the atmosphere's transmittance LUT. Sky irradiance illuminates clouds; two attenuated scattering orders approximate cloud multiple scattering. Cloud shadows attenuate ground sunlight. Representative-depth aerial perspective adds atmospheric extinction and in-scattering between the camera and the clouds.

This is an approximate real-time model, not a fully coupled atmosphere/cloud multiple-scattering solver. Clouds occupy a spherical shell at 1.8–7.5 km. The atmosphere uses the original 6360 km model radius and Air's uniform dark-blue ground albedo. Orbit reuses Air's Orbit preset, surface-target camera, 50-degree field of view with the same portrait adjustment, sunlight and exposure. Its distance control measures distance from the surface target, matching Air, rather than altitude above the spherical ground. Cloudscape and Sunset retain their local horizon cameras. Air's 1 km demo sphere is not included.

Optimization: the shared Air LUT URLs use browser caching; a 64³ RGBA8 periodic cloud texture (about 1.14 MiB including mipmaps) is generated once on the GPU instead of evaluating Worley neighborhoods in every march sample. Distance-based mip selection filters subpixel cloud details in orbital views. Empty shell intervals are skipped, opaque rays terminate early, and balanced mode uses 36 view steps with a 480k-pixel cap (high: 64 / 950k). Animation is limited to 24 fps, stops in hidden tabs, and can be paused for rendering only on input/resize. Reduced-motion preference defaults to paused. GPU objects, animation callbacks, observers and fetches are cleaned up on navigation.

Atmosphere shaders and LUTs: © Eric Bruneton, BSD 3-Clause, license preserved at `public/air/bruneton/LICENSE`. Noise approach: Sébastien Hillaire with weirdfoo, TileableVolumeNoise (MIT), Worley method credited upstream to Marc-André Loyer.
